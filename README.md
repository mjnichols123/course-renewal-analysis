# Course Renewal Analysis

Exploratory data analysis of customer ordering and email outreach behavior around course expiration dates for Pacific Medical Training.

## Project Overview

This project investigates how customer ordering behavior relates to course expiration dates and email outreach. The goal is to identify patterns that may help improve the timing and frequency of renewal-related customer communication.

The analysis focuses on several questions:

- When do customers place orders relative to course expiration?
- How does pre-expiration email outreach relate to subsequent ordering?
- How many pre-expiration emails are associated with the strongest order rates?
- Does the timing of outreach before expiration matter?
- How long after receiving an email do customers tend to place an order?
- How is ordering activity distributed during the 30 days before and after expiration?
- Are there identifiable periods in the renewal lifecycle where customer activity is especially concentrated?

This repository is designed to provide a reproducible analysis pipeline so future analysts can rerun, validate, and extend the work as additional questions or data become available.

---

## Data Source

The analysis uses the public `published_data` files from the Hugging Face dataset:

`fulldecent/mts_course_renewal_marketing`

The project loads the data programmatically rather than requiring the source data files to be manually stored in this repository.

Three primary tables are used.

### `email_blasts.parquet`

Contains customer-level email outreach records and course expiration information associated with those email records.

Important fields include:

- `sent_at`
- `is_large_blast`
- `email_blinded_index`
- course expiration date fields

### `expirations.parquet`

Contains course expiration events.

Important fields include:

- `email_blinded_index`
- `expired_date`
- `course_blinded_index`
- `our_course`

### `orders.parquet`

Contains customer order records.

Important fields include:

- `created_at`
- `email_blinded_index`
- `price`

Customer and course identifiers are blinded.

### Important Order Data Limitation

The orders table identifies the customer placing an order but does not identify the specific course purchased.

As a result, this project measures **subsequent customer ordering around a course expiration event**, not confirmed renewal of the specific expiring course.

References to "order rates" throughout the project should be interpreted with this distinction in mind.

## Current Exploratory Findings

The project is exploratory. The findings below describe observed associations and should not be interpreted as causal effects.

### 1. Pre-Expiration Outreach and Subsequent Ordering

Expiration events receiving pre-expiration outreach generally show higher subsequent customer order rates than expiration events receiving no pre-expiration outreach.

This establishes an association between outreach and subsequent ordering, but does not establish that the emails caused the additional orders.

### 2. Pre-Expiration Email Frequency

The most notable increase in observed order rates occurs between one and two pre-expiration emails.

In the aggregate analysis:

- zero and one email have relatively similar longer-term order rates;
- two emails are associated with substantially stronger subsequent ordering;
- three emails and four-or-more emails show relatively limited additional improvement over two emails.

This suggests a possible diminishing-return pattern after approximately two emails.

Email frequency should not yet be interpreted independently of email timing because customers receiving more emails may also begin receiving outreach earlier.

### 3. Pre-Expiration Email Timing

Outreach timing appears to matter, particularly during the final month before expiration.

When expiration events are assigned to mutually exclusive groups based on their first email during the final 30 days, the observed rate of a subsequent order during the final seven days before expiration is:

| First Email Timing | Expiration Events | Final-Week Orders | Final-Week Order Rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0–7 days | 1,250 | 3 | 0.24% |
| 8–14 days | 940 | 23 | 2.45% |
| 15–21 days | 1,736 | 77 | 4.44% |
| 22–30 days | 4,897 | 269 | 5.49% |

The strongest observed final-week order rate occurs when outreach begins 22–30 days before expiration, followed by 15–21 days.

The pattern suggests that beginning outreach approximately two to four weeks before expiration may be more favorable than waiting until the final week.

These results remain observational and should not be interpreted as evidence that earlier emails cause customers to order.

### 4. Email-to-Order Delay

The analysis connects pre-expiration emails occurring during the final 30 days to the next subsequent customer order occurring within 30 days of the email.

There are 2,391 matched email-to-order observations.

The observed delay from email to order is:

- Mean: approximately **13.01 days**
- Median: approximately **12.63 days**
- 0–3 days: **17.31%**
- 4–7 days: **16.06%**
- 8–14 days: **27.23%**
- 15–21 days: **21.04%**
- 22–30 days: **18.36%**

The largest individual group occurs 8–14 days after outreach.

These results suggest that subsequent ordering following outreach is frequently delayed rather than immediate. A customer may receive an email and not place an order until one or more weeks later.

The percentages above describe the timing distribution **among emails that were followed by an order within 30 days**. They should not be interpreted as the probability that an individual email produces an order.

### 5. Ordering Around Expiration

A focused ±30-day analysis identified **10,452 expiration events** with a nearby customer order.

Among these events:

- **69.14%** of the nearest orders occurred before expiration.
- **30.86%** occurred on or after expiration.
- The median nearest order occurred approximately **4.83 days before expiration**.
- The final seven days before expiration contained the largest concentration of nearby orders.
- **69.14%** of nearby orders had occurred by expiration.
- **82.65%** had occurred by seven days after expiration.
- **89.53%** had occurred by fourteen days after expiration.
- **94.60%** had occurred by twenty-one days after expiration.

The order distribution and cumulative distribution analyses indicate that customer ordering becomes increasingly concentrated as expiration approaches and is particularly concentrated immediately before expiration.

### 6. Combined Timing Pattern

Several analyses point toward a consistent customer-behavior pattern:

1. Beginning outreach earlier in the final month is associated with stronger final-week ordering.
2. Matched subsequent orders occur a median of approximately 12.6 days after outreach.
3. Customer ordering becomes particularly concentrated during the final week before expiration.
4. Beginning outreach only during the final week is associated with the weakest observed final-week order rate.

Together, these findings suggest that beginning outreach roughly **two to four weeks before expiration** may provide customers sufficient time to act while still keeping communication close to the expiration deadline.

This should be treated as a working hypothesis rather than a causal conclusion.

## Important Analysis Definitions

### Expiration Event

Where appropriate, an expiration event is uniquely identified using:

- `email_blinded_index`
- `expired_date`
- `course_blinded_index`

This definition should be maintained unless an alternative definition is explicitly documented.

### Subsequent Order

A subsequent order means that the same customer placed an order after the event being analyzed.

Because the purchased course is not available in the orders table, a subsequent order is **not necessarily a renewal of the expiring course**.

### ±30-Day Expiration Window

The focused expiration-window analysis examines customer orders occurring within 30 days before or after expiration.

When multiple orders are available, the nearest order to each expiration event is selected for the primary distribution analysis.

### Email Timing

Email timing is measured relative to the expiration date.

Some exploratory timing analyses allow an expiration event to appear in multiple groups if the customer received emails during multiple timing periods.

The first-email analysis avoids this overlap by assigning each expiration event to one mutually exclusive timing group based on the first observed email during the final 30 days.

### Final-Week Order

The final-week analysis identifies a subsequent customer order occurring after the relevant email and during the final seven days before expiration.

## Reproducibility Notes

Several considerations are important when reproducing or extending the analysis:

- The source dataset is retrieved programmatically from the published Hugging Face dataset.
- Customer and course identifiers are blinded.
- Expiration events are deduplicated using customer, expiration date, and course identifiers where appropriate.
- Analyses involving orders around expiration should clearly define whether they use all possible order-expiration pairs or a single nearest order per expiration event.
- The ±30-day distribution analysis selects the nearest order within the window for each expiration event.
- Some email timing groups overlap when an expiration event receives emails during multiple periods.
- The first-email timing analysis uses mutually exclusive groups.
- Email frequency and email timing may be related to each other.
- Reported relationships between outreach and ordering are observational.
- Important findings should be written to generated reports rather than existing only in terminal output.

These definitions should be preserved or explicitly documented when future analyses modify them.

## Known Limitations

The most important limitations of the current analysis are:

### Orders Cannot Be Linked to Specific Courses

The largest limitation is that the orders table does not contain the course associated with the purchase.

Therefore, this project cannot directly determine whether a customer renewed the specific course that expired.

### Observational Analysis

Customers are not randomly assigned to email strategies.

Differences in order rates may reflect:

- customer characteristics
- course characteristics
- previous customer behavior
- email timing
- email frequency
- campaign type
- other unobserved factors

The current analysis should therefore be used to identify patterns and hypotheses rather than establish causal effects.

### Timing and Frequency May Interact

Customers whose outreach begins earlier may also have more opportunities to receive additional emails.

The observed benefit associated with earlier outreach may therefore partly reflect email frequency.

This interaction is the highest-priority area for continued analysis.

## Continuing the Analysis

The current analysis establishes several useful descriptive relationships between email outreach, expiration timing, and subsequent customer ordering.

The highest-priority next step is to determine how **email timing and email frequency interact**.

Current results separately suggest that:

- two emails are associated with substantially stronger order rates than one email;
- additional emails beyond two show relatively limited aggregate improvement;
- outreach beginning approximately 15–30 days before expiration is associated with stronger final-week ordering;
- matched email-to-order observations have a median delay of approximately 12.6 days.

However, customers receiving earlier outreach may also receive more emails later. Timing and frequency should therefore not yet be treated as independent effects.

### Recommended Next Analyses

#### 1. Email Frequency × Timing Interaction

Compare outcomes for customers whose first email occurs 15–30 days before expiration while accounting for whether they receive:

- one total email
- two total emails
- three or more total emails

This is the highest-priority recommended continuation of the current work.

#### 2. Large vs. Non-Large Email Blasts

Use `is_large_blast` to determine whether observed relationships differ between large campaign sends and more targeted outreach.

#### 3. Course-Level Analysis

Examine whether email and ordering patterns differ across `course_blinded_index`.

This could help identify whether the aggregate results are driven primarily by particular courses.

#### 4. Post-Expiration Behavior

Extend the detailed weekly analysis into the first 30 days after expiration and investigate whether different outreach strategies are associated with post-expiration ordering.

#### 5. Robustness Checks

Test whether the major findings remain under alternative:

- duplicate handling
- order-matching definitions
- expiration-event definitions
- analysis windows

#### 6. Course-Level Renewal Validation

If future data identifies the course associated with each order, repeat the analysis using confirmed course renewals rather than general subsequent customer orders.

## Analysis Principles

When extending this project:

1. Preserve the original published data.
2. Perform transformations programmatically.
3. Keep substantial analysis logic in reusable functions within `src/`.
4. Keep `analysis.py` as the main orchestration and entry-point script.
5. Document important population and denominator definitions.
6. Clearly distinguish subsequent orders from confirmed renewals.
7. Save generated figures to `reports/figures/`.
8. Record important findings and limitations in the generated Markdown reports.
9. Avoid interpreting observational relationships as causal without appropriate evidence.
10. Keep the complete workflow reproducible from the repository.
